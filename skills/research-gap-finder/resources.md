# 🔬 The Ultimate List of Learning Resources for Finding & Extracting Real Research Gaps

## I. Foundational Frameworks & Methodologies

1. **PICO/PICOS Framework** — Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, (Setting): a structured vocabulary for precisely characterizing a gap. Most organizations use PICO as a method of identifying research gaps; a worksheet helps organize them.
2. **Six-Type Knowledge Gap Taxonomy** — evidence gaps, methodological gaps, population gaps, contextual gaps, theoretical gaps, translational gaps. Each type has identifying features and the forms of evidence needed to establish it as a *genuine* absence rather than an artifact of incomplete searching.
3. **Five-Dimension Importance Rubric** — theoretical importance, practical importance, feasibility, novelty, coherence with research trajectory. Importance assessment is explicitly distinguished from gap identification (many gaps are genuine but unimportant).
4. **AHRQ Framework for Determining Research Gaps** — gold-standard, published by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality: `ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK126702/`
5. **Robinson et al. Framework (Johns Hopkins EPC)** — frameworks for determining research gaps during systematic reviews, examining EPCs and organizations that conduct systematic reviews.
6. **Arksey-O'Malley Scoping Review Framework** — widely used for mapping the breadth of a field and finding where research is thin.

## II. AI & Technology Tools

7. **Litmaps** (litmaps.com) — citation-network analysis; "reference gaps" are the easiest way to spot gaps in an unfamiliar field.
8. **ResearchRabbit** (researchrabbit.ai) — AI visualization of connections between papers; surfaces disconnections keyword search misses.
9. **Connected Papers** (connectedpapers.com) — interactive citation networks and concept maps to "see" how papers interrelate.
10. **Elicit** (elicit.com) — LLM-automated search, summarization, and data extraction; finds papers without perfect keyword match.
11. **SciSpace** (scispace.com) — semantic literature search; AI analyses abstracts/full-text for plain-text queries.
12. **Consensus** (consensus.app) — semantic AI search extracting evidence-based answers from 200M+ papers; what has/hasn't been studied.
13. **AnswerThis – Research Gap Finder** (answerthis.io) — identifies unexplored areas, contradictions, and knowledge gaps worth investigating.
14. **Aveksana** — AI assistant for research topics and gap analysis; calculates research potential as a percentage, topic comparison, topic locking.
15. **Semantic Scholar** (semanticscholar.org) — AI search with TLDR summaries, citation-influence scores, recommendations.
16. **Inciteful** (inciteful.xyz) — citation-network graphs from one paper; important papers in a field; shortest citation path between two papers.
17. **Scite.ai** (scite.ai) — how papers were cited: supported, contradicted, or merely mentioned — great for finding contradictions (a gap type).
18. **Dimensions** (dimensions.ai) — maps research landscapes alongside PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar.

## IV. Practical Strategies & Techniques

27. **Read "Limitations" & "Future Directions" sections** — often point directly to areas needing exploration.
28. **Read "Future Research" sections** — authors name gaps they couldn't address, populations excluded, questions that emerged.
29. **Start with systematic reviews & meta-analyses** — they flag unresolved questions; 3–5 recent ones beat dozens of primary studies.
30. **Reference Gap Method (Litmaps)** — review papers forgetting key references reveals gaps.
31. **Snowballing** — follow citations; hybrid keyword+author strategies; Boolean operators.
32. **Citation tracking (forward & backward)** — ScienceDirect, Web of Science, Wiley list articles citing the one you're reading.
33. **Cross-population / cross-context comparison** — emerging tech not yet applied to traditional problems; inconsistencies across populations/contexts/timelines.
34. **Summary/Evidence Matrix method** — broad question → initial searches → synthesize in a matrix → stakeholder perspectives → iterate.
35. **Visual mind mapping** — map the literature; an unanswered mapped question is a probable gap.
36. **Four Causes of Gaps** — insufficient/imprecise info; biased info; inconsistent/unknown results; wrong-kind-of-information.
37. **Interdisciplinary intersection** — disconnections between fields using different terminology for the same concept (keyword search misses these).

## XII. Key Academic Papers on Gap Identification

86. **Robinson et al. (2011)** — "Frameworks for Determining Research Gaps During Systematic Reviews" (AHRQ). A research gap = a topic/area where missing or inadequate information limits the ability of reviewers to reach a conclusion.
87. **Müller-Bloch & Kranz (2015)** — "A Framework for Rigorously Identifying Research Gaps in Qualitative Literature Reviews" (ICIS).
88. **Nyanchoka et al. (2019)** — "Use of Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses to Identify Research Gaps" (BMJ Open).

## ✅ Quick-Start Checklist

| # | Action |
|---|--------|
| 1 | Pick 3–5 recent **systematic reviews** in the area |
| 2 | Read every **"Limitations"** and **"Future Directions"** section |
| 3 | Upload references to **Litmaps** → find **reference gaps** |
| 4 | Use **ResearchRabbit** → visualize **citation disconnections** |
| 5 | Use **Elicit/Consensus** → ask natural-language questions about what's unstudied |
| 6 | Build an **evidence matrix** mapping Study → Method → Population → Findings → Gaps |
| 7 | Apply the **six-type gap taxonomy** to classify each gap |
| 8 | Apply the **five-dimension importance rubric** to rank gaps |
| 9 | Cross-check with **grant databases** and **preprints** for novelty |
| 10 | Formulate the **research question** from the most promising gap |

> 💡 **Pro tip:** many gaps are genuine but unimportant — distinguish *gap identification* from *importance assessment*. Validate against real-world relevance, theoretical significance, and feasibility before committing.
